Comparison with our 64^3/96^3 runs (paper/window_tests, paper/offset_tests):
- Window drift: beta 0.55-0.65 here vs 0.46-0.64 there. Random-walk-like in both; lag-1 correlation positive in both (+0.2..+0.34 here, ~+0.5 there).
- Small-separation exponent: gamma_high = 0.58 here vs 0.26-0.40 there. Here it is above 1/2, but the offsets are near-dissipative-range
  scales (1-8 dx = 2-17 eta), where smooth Taylor behaviour (gamma -> 1) is expected. So this does not show a gain uniform in Re.
- Holder quotient (box units) is larger here (median 2.6-3.4 at the end) because sqrt(r) is much smaller; at offset 1 it nearly doubled
  over 60 windows (1.37 -> 2.56), while it fell at offsets 4 and 8. Comparisons of absolute quotient values across resolutions are not meaningful.
Caveats: one time origin only; omega_ref is a sampled lower bound, so windows may be too long; seeds come from the
top 64 samples, not true near-max points; pairs leave the high set fast (<=8% both-high at the end); short duration.

Conclusions / caveats:
- Offsets 1-8 cells = 2-17 eta are near-dissipative scales where smooth (Taylor, gamma -> 1) behaviour is expected;
  even so gamma_high is mostly ~1/2 or below. Nothing here indicates a gain uniform in Re; one Re only.
- 60 windows ~ 0.12-0.17 time units per run; five start times of ONE statistically stationary forced flow
  (not independent flows, not near-singular).
- omega_ref is a sampled lower bound (see table). High-set statistics thin out quickly at larger offsets.
- This is numerical evidence only; it proves nothing and does not bear on any claim of regularity.
