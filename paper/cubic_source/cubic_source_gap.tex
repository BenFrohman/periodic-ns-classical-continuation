\begin{abstract}
For a smooth divergence-free solution of the unforced periodic Navier--Stokes equations, let $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$. At a spatial maximum of $|\omega|$, transport vanishes and viscosity only lowers $M$, so $M'\le \omega\cdot S\omega/M$. The 7 October version claimed $\omega\cdot S\omega\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}}M^3$ from Calder\'on--Zygmund theory. That step is \emph{not} proved: it needs $\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$, which Calder\'on--Zygmund theory does not give. This corrected version replaces it by the classical log-corrected bound, and separately records the uncorrected inequality as a named hypothesis. The comparison ODEs, the ceiling under a quartic sink $\kappa M^4$, and the implication from that sink to a finite Beale--Kato--Majda integral are checked in Lean. The sink itself remains open. Numerics at the vorticity maximum are reported and show no sign of a sink.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
Let $u$ be a smooth divergence-free solution of the unforced periodic Navier--Stokes equations on $\mathbb{T}^3$, with viscosity $\nu>0$, and let $\omega=\nabla\times u$, $S=\tfrac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top})$. Write $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$. The vorticity equation is
\[
\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=(\omega\cdot\nabla)u+\nu\Delta\omega,
\qquad \omega\cdot(\omega\cdot\nabla)u=\omega\cdot S\omega .
\]

\section{The pairing at the maximum, and the corrected source bound}
\paragraph{Proved (classical).} At a spatial maximum $x$ of $|\omega|$, $\omega\cdot(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=\tfrac12u\cdot\nabla|\omega|^2=0$, and $\omega\cdot\Delta\omega=\tfrac12\Delta|\omega|^2-|\nabla\omega|^2\le0$. Hence, in the sense of upper Dini derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing}
M\,M'\le \omega(x)\cdot S(x)\omega(x)\le \|S\|_{L^\infty}M^2 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The inconsistency in the 7 October version.} That version stated that Biot--Savart is a Calder\'on--Zygmund operator bounded from $L^\infty$ to $\mathrm{BMO}$ and \emph{not} on $L^\infty$, and in the next line used $\omega\cdot S\omega\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}}M^3$. The second statement requires $\|S\|_{L^\infty}\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}}\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$, which is exactly the $L^\infty$ bound the first statement denies. The Riesz transforms are unbounded on $L^\infty$, so the bound $M'\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}}M^2$ was not proved and is withdrawn as a proved statement.

\paragraph{Replacement (a): the log-corrected bound (classical, not formalized here).} The Beale--Kato--Majda / Kato--Ponce estimate gives, for $s>3/2$,
\[
\|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty}\le C\,M\Bigl(1+\log^+\frac{\|\omega\|_{H^s}}{M}\Bigr)+C\|\omega\|_{L^2},
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logsource}
M'\le C M^2\Bigl(1+\log^+\frac{\|\omega\|_{H^s}}{M}\Bigr)+C\|\omega\|_{L^2}M .
\end{equation}
The logarithm involves a higher norm, which is controlled only through the BKM integral itself; \eqref{eq:logsource} is therefore an ingredient of the BKM argument, not a closed ODE for $M$.

\paragraph{Replacement (b): conditional, under a named hypothesis.}
\begin{definition}[\texttt{PeriodicStrainLinfBound}, false]
There is $C\ge0$ with $\|S(t,x)\|\le C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ for all smooth maximal solutions, all $t$ below the lifespan, and all $x$.
\end{definition}
This hypothesis is \emph{false} for every $\nu>0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:refuted}); it is recorded only to show what it would have given. Under it, $\omega\cdot S\omega\le CM^3$ (Lean: \texttt{pairing\_le\_of\_periodicStrainLinfBound}) and therefore $M'\le CM^2$.

\begin{remark}[\texttt{PeriodicStrainLinfBound} is false]\label{rem:refuted}
For every $\nu>0$ there is no $C\ge0$ with $\|S(t,x)\|\le C\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ along all smooth maximal solutions (Lean: \texttt{not\_periodicStrainLinfBound}, file \texttt{StrainLinfRefutation.lean}).
\end{remark}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (static failure).} Let $f_K(s)=\frac4\pi\sum_{i<N}\frac{\sin(2\pi(2i+1)s)}{2i+1}$, $K=2N-1$, and let $u_K$ be the smooth, divergence-free, $\mathbb Z^3$-periodic, $z$-independent field with $\omega_K=\operatorname{curl}u_K=f_K(x)f_K(y)\,e_z$. Then $|f_K|\le 6$, so $\|\omega_K\|_{L^\infty}\le36$ for all $K$ (Lean: \texttt{vorticity\_sup\_norm\_uK\_le}; numerically $\|\omega_K\|_\infty\le1.403$ for odd $K\ge7$). The Fourier identity gives
\[
S_{11}(u_K)(0)=\frac{16}{\pi^2}\sum_{i,j<N}\frac{1}{(2i+1)^2+(2j+1)^2}\ \ge\ \frac{2}{\pi^2}\log(N+1)
\]
(Lean: \texttt{strain00\_origin}, \texttt{bcSum\_ge\_log}), and the sum diverges (\texttt{bcSum\_unbounded}); asymptotically $\|S(u_K)(0)\|\sim\frac2\pi\log K$. Hence no $C$ satisfies $\|S(u)(x)\|\le C\|\omega(u)\|_{L^\infty}$ for all smooth periodic divergence-free $u$ (\texttt{not\_staticStrainLinfBound}).

\emph{Step 2 (from data to solutions).} Suppose the solution-level bound held with constant $C$. Take any smooth periodic datum $u_0$. Local existence gives $T^*(u_0)>0$ (\texttt{periodicLocalExistence\_of\_nonneg}), and uniqueness gives a maximal solution $u$ (\texttt{periodicMaximalSolution\_of\_uniqueness}, \texttt{periodicEnergyDifferenceEstimate}). Choose $0<T<T^*$; the solution on $[0,T]$ satisfies $u(0)=u_0$. The bound at $t=0$ then reads $\|S(u_0)(x)\|\le C\|\omega(u_0)\|_{L^\infty}$, i.e.\ the static bound, contradicting Step~1 (Lean: \texttt{not\_periodicStrainLinfBound\_of\_static}).
\end{proof}
All inputs are theorems in the repository; no hypothesis Prop is used, and \texttt{\#print axioms} reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}. Only the sharp Gibbs constant $\approx1.39$ is numerical, and it is not needed. The counterexample is classical (Bahouri--Chemin type); the explicit periodic version is published as B.~S.~Frohman, \emph{Is $\|S\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$? A deep check of \texttt{PeriodicStrainLinfBound}}, Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23904623 (Lean: \texttt{PeriodicNSContinuation/StrainLinf.lean}, commit \texttt{216ba1c}). The refutation concerns only the $t=0$ uniform bound; the $\nu,t$-dependent variant for $t>0$ is a different statement and is not addressed here.

\section{What the ODEs give (proved in Lean)}
All statements in this section are abstract real-analysis lemmas in \texttt{CubicSource.lean}, with no \texttt{sorry} and only the standard axioms.
\begin{proposition}[\texttt{quadratic\_comparison}, \texttt{lifespan\_lower\_bound}]
If $M>0$ is differentiable and $M'\le CM^2$, then $1/M(t)\ge 1/M_0-Ct$, so $M(t)\le M_0/(1-CM_0t)$ for $CM_0t<1$.
\end{proposition}
This is a lifespan lower bound $T^*\ge 1/(CM_0)$ only. The comparison solution $y_0/(1-Cy_0t)$ blows up at $1/(Cy_0)$, so this cannot close BKM.
\begin{proposition}[\texttt{cubic\_comparison}]
If $C\ge0$ and $M'\le CM^2(1+\log M)$, then $1/M(t)^2\ge 1/M_0^2-2Ct$.
\end{proposition}
Again only a lifespan lower bound: $\int^\infty dM/(M^2\log M)<\infty$, so even a self-referential log correction does not prevent finite-time blowup of the comparison.
\begin{proposition}[\texttt{fence\_ceiling}, \texttt{sink\_ceiling}]
If $M$ is continuous with right derivative $M'\le M^2(C-\kappa M)$, $\kappa>0$, then $M\le\max(M_0,C/\kappa)$.
\end{proposition}

\section{The open gap: the quartic sink}
\begin{definition}[\texttt{PeriodicQuarticVorticitySink}, open, never assumed]
For every smooth maximal solution there are $C$ and $\kappa>0$ such that, below the lifespan, $M$ is continuous with right derivative $M'\le M^2(C-\kappa M)$; equivalently, at the maximum, $\omega\cdot S\omega+\nu\,\omega\cdot\Delta\omega\le CM^3-\kappa M^4$.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[\texttt{bkm\_bound\_of\_quarticSink}, proved]
The quartic sink gives $c$ with $M\le c$ and $\int_0^T M\,dt\le cT$ for every $T$ below the lifespan. In particular the BKM integral is finite on $[0,T^*)$ whenever $T^*<\infty$, which by Beale--Kato--Majda would contradict finite lifespan.
\end{theorem}
The sink is not derived from the unmodified vorticity equation. It is at least as strong as a uniform bound on the vorticity maximum, i.e.\ it is the problem restated as an inequality.

\section{Three false fixes (unchanged)}
(i) A formal certificate that expands the assumed rate $M(CM-\kappa M^2)$ into $CM^2-\kappa M^3$ is ring arithmetic on a hypothesis. (ii) The weight $1+\kappa M^2$ turns the source into $CM^3+C\kappa M^5$: a degree-five source, not a sink. (iii) The logarithmic repair needs control of $\|\omega\|_{H^s}$ and is an integral criterion, not a derivation of the sink.

\paragraph{Reading.} (1) $\|S\|_{L^\infty}/M$ stays in about $[0.33,0.61]$ over the observed range. So a ratio bound holds on these particular flows; the uniform bound \texttt{PeriodicStrainLinfBound} is nonetheless false in general (Remark~\ref{rem:refuted}). (2) $\sigma/M^3$ is mostly positive, with a maximum of $0.12$--$0.21$ per run. Fitting $\log(\sigma/M^3)$ against $\log M$ over the positive snapshots gives slopes between $-0.84$ and $-0.13$. So the source is roughly cubic, or somewhat weaker than cubic, at the maximum. (3) $\sigma$ is negative at some snapshots (0--22\% of them; vortex compression at the maximum). These snapshots are intermittent: the fraction in the top quartile of $M$ (0--20\%) is comparable to the overall fraction, with no systematic trend with $M$. A sink $\kappa M^4$ would require $\sigma/M^3\le C-\kappa M$ at every large $M$; the data show no such ordering. (4) $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is nonnegative only by the choice of $C_{\mathrm{run}}$. Its minimum over the top quartile shrinks toward $0$ (down to $4\times10^{-4}$ at the finest run), which is the opposite of a uniform $\kappa>0$. (5) The viscous term at the maximum is negative, of order $10$--$35\,\nu M^2$: a quadratic, not quartic, sink. \emph{Caveats:} grids $64^3$ and $96^3$, all six runs complete to $t=8$; peak vorticity only up to about $50$; one or two seeds; decaying, unforced flow; snapshots every $0.05$; the $\arg\max$ is taken on the grid. Nothing here bears on behaviour near a putative singularity.

\section{Record}
\textbf{Proved:} the pairing \eqref{eq:pairing}; the comparison lemmas; the sink ceiling; sink $\Rightarrow$ finite BKM integral. \textbf{Refuted (proved in Lean):} \texttt{PeriodicStrainLinfBound} (Remark~\ref{rem:refuted}), so the conditional $M'\le CM^2$ under it is vacuous as a route; the log-corrected source \eqref{eq:logsource} is classical and not formalized here. \textbf{Open:} \texttt{PeriodicQuarticVorticitySink}. No theorem in this note proves global regularity for the periodic Navier--Stokes equations.
